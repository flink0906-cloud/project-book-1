Information security and cyber law

Information security involves safeguarding information and computer systems against unauthorized access, misuse, alteration, loss, damage, or destruction. Since computers and the internet are widely used in areas such as communication, education, banking, commerce, and other daily activities, ensuring the security of digital information has become increasingly important. Along with technical protection, computer ethics focuses on the moral responsibilities of technology users. This includes respecting other people's privacy, avoiding unauthorized access, and using computer resources in a responsible and appropriate manner.

Cybercrime consists of unlawful activities carried out through computers, digital networks, or the internet. Hacking, identity theft, online fraud, cyberbullying, unauthorized system access, and the theft of digital information are common examples. Such activities may be intended to obtain confidential information, disrupt systems, or gain financial advantages. To control these offenses and protect individuals and organizations, governments establish cyber laws and regulations. Another major security concern is malware, which refers to harmful software designed to disrupt systems or perform unauthorized actions. Viruses, worms, Trojan horses, spyware, and ransomware are among its common forms.

Phishing is a cyber threat in which attackers impersonate trustworthy individuals or organizations to trick users into revealing sensitive information. Similarly, spam consists of unwanted messages that are generally distributed to large numbers of users and may sometimes contain harmful links or attachments. Encryption provides an important method of protecting digital information by transforming readable data into a coded form that is difficult for unauthorized users to understand. The reverse process, known as decryption, restores the information to its original readable form. Along with encryption, strong passwords, secure networks, and careful management of personal information contribute significantly to maintaining data security.

A combination of security tools and responsible practices can reduce the risks associated with cyber threats. Firewalls monitor and regulate network traffic, helping to block unauthorized connections, while antivirus programs identify and remove many forms of malicious software. Keeping operating systems and applications updated is also essential because updates frequently address known security vulnerabilities. Users should avoid suspicious websites, links, and files, create strong passwords, and maintain regular backups of important data. Backups are particularly valuable because they make it possible to restore information when the original files are accidentally deleted, corrupted, damaged, or lost.

Information security is also connected with the protection of intellectual property and the authenticity of electronic information. Intellectual Property Rights safeguard original creations and innovations, with copyright covering works such as books, music, photographs, and software, patents protecting inventions and technological developments, and trademarks covering distinctive names, logos, and symbols. Digital signatures can be used to verify the identity of a sender and establish the authenticity of electronic documents or transactions. Cyber law provides the legal framework for activities involving computers, digital systems, and the internet. In Nepal, laws such as the Electronic Transactions Act regulate various electronic activities and provide legal recognition to certain digital transactions. ICT policies further guide the development and responsible use of information and communication technologies, making knowledge of security, ethics, and cyber law essential for safe and lawful digital participation.
